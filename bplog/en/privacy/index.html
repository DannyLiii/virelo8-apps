Privacy Policy

BP Notebook collects no personal data at all. There's no account, no ads and no tracking, and the app never sends your data to a server.

  • No account
  • No data collected
  • No tracking or ads
  • Stays on your iPhone

Who this policy covers

This policy covers the iPhone app BP Notebook (the app), made by the independent developer LI YAN-SHAN.

Data kept on your iPhone

The blood pressure, pulse, time and time of day, tags (arm, posture, before or after a meal, which monitor), the reasons you tick when a reading is far from usual, notes, doctor's notes (your text and photos), the birth year, height and weight you enter, names of the people you record for, reminders and settings you enter are kept only in the app's storage on your iPhone. A report's header prints your birth year, which others see only when you share the report. The app keeps one automatic backup a day on the same iPhone for 14 days.

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, this data is included in your own device backup as iOS is set up to do. We have no access to your iPhone or your backups.

Setup questions

To set the text size, the home screen and tips, the app asks a few questions the first time you open it: your age group, whether and when you take blood pressure medicine, and health conditions (diabetes, kidney disease, heart failure or a fluid limit from your doctor, atrial fibrillation, stroke or heart disease, pregnancy or the 6 weeks after giving birth). You can skip any question.

Your answers stay on your iPhone and in backup files you export yourself. They don't appear in notifications, widgets or CSV files, the app doesn't send them anywhere, and we can't see them. You can change or clear them at any time in Settings › My situation and screen. If you answer for a family member, let them know first.

Doctor's notes

You can write down what your doctor told you: your target, how to measure, medicine names and how to take them, what to watch for, your next visit and clinic, and questions to ask. You can also add up to 6 photos (a note from your doctor, a medicine bag, a handout). These stay on your iPhone only. They aren't shown in notifications or widgets, and the app doesn't send them anywhere. The first page of a report prints them under "Doctor's notes (written down by the patient; please check)", which others see only when you share the report. The app makes no decisions based on them and doesn't remind you to take medicine.

Apple Health

The app reads or writes Health data only after you allow it, and you can allow just some types.

  • Read: blood pressure and heart rate; and, if you choose to use them in reports, trends or suggestions, weight, height, date of birth (for your age), resting heart rate, heart rate variability, blood oxygen, sleep, steps, exercise and mindful minutes, sodium, potassium, caffeine, alcohol, hypertension notifications and the medications you pick.
  • Written: the blood pressure and heart rate you log in the app. Readings imported from a CSV file are never written to Health.

If you use reminders, the app reads new blood pressure readings from Health in the background, only to tell whether that time of day has been measured and a reminder is still needed.

This data is used only on your iPhone, to show your readings, make reports and give suggestions. It is never used for advertising, never shared with anyone and never uploaded. You can turn access off at any time in Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices.

Camera and photos

When you tap "Photograph the blood pressure monitor", the app uses the camera (asking the first time) to read the numbers on your monitor's display. The photo of your monitor is read on your iPhone by its built-in Apple Intelligence; it is never uploaded or kept. Photos you add to Doctor's notes (a note from your doctor, a medicine bag, a handout) are kept in the app, on this iPhone only. If you pick a photo instead, the app reads only the one you pick, without access to your photo library, and uses the time it was taken as the time of the reading.

Siri and Shortcuts

You can log a reading with Siri or the Shortcuts app. When you use Siri, what you say is processed by Apple's Siri under Apple's privacy policy; the app only receives the recognized numbers, keeps them on your iPhone and doesn't send them anywhere. Your iPhone has to be unlocked to add a reading.

Reminders

Notifications and alarms are scheduled by iOS on your iPhone and don't pass through any server.

Reports, exports and sharing

PDF reports, CSV files and backup files are made on your iPhone. They leave it only when you share them yourself, with whoever you choose; after that, the recipient's own practices apply.

The "Blood pressure summary" picture is also made on your iPhone and leaves it only when you share it. Saving it to Photos asks for permission to add photos; the app only adds that picture and never reads your photos.

Purchases

Apple handles Pro subscriptions. We never receive your name, payment details or Apple Account. The app only asks Apple whether you have Pro. Apple's privacy policy explains how Apple handles purchase data.

Crashes and diagnostics

If you choose to share with app developers in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, Apple gives us crash reports and usage statistics that don't identify you. We use them only to fix problems.

Deleting your data

  • Settings > Export and backup > Delete all readings in the app deletes one person's readings. Today's automatic backup keeps them for a while so the deletion can be undone.
  • Deleting the app deletes all of its data on your iPhone, automatic backups included.
  • Readings already written to Health are deleted in the Health app.

Children

The app is made for adults keeping track of their blood pressure. We don't collect anyone's data, children's included.

Your rights

As we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing to access, correct or delete. If you have any question about privacy, please write to us.

Changes

If the way the app handles data changes, we'll update this page and the date below first. If the app ever starts collecting any data, it will tell you and ask for your consent first.
